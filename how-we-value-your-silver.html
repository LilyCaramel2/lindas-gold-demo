How We Value Your Silver: Bullion, Coins and Jewellery

If you hold silver coins, bars or jewellery and want to know how we value silver bullion and coins in Fish Hoek, this guide breaks down the main factors so you can walk into Linda’s Gold with confidence.

Why Silver Valuation Matters

Silver is traded globally, used in industry, and often held as savings in coins, bars, jewellery and silverware. When you bring silver to Linda’s Gold, it is important to understand how the value is calculated rather than receiving a single unexplained offer.

Transparent valuation is central to our approach. Whether you own a single silver bar or a mixed collection of coins and jewellery, each item deserves clear, practical assessment.

Step 1: Identifying the Type of Silver

The first step is to identify what kind of silver you are holding. Common categories include investment-grade bullion bars, modern bullion coins, older circulation coins that contain silver, jewellery and tableware such as spoons or serving pieces.

Each type has its own typical purity range and context. Identifying the category allows us to apply the correct testing and pricing method from the start.

Step 2: Checking Purity and Hallmarks

Silver bullion bars and coins usually indicate their purity clearly, using marks such as “999”, “999.9” or “925”. Jewellery or tableware may carry hallmarks on the inside of a ring, the back of a pendant or the underside of a fork or spoon.

At our premises in Triangle Square, 5 De Waal Road, Fish Hoek, these markings can be examined in front of you. Where stamps are unclear or missing, we use additional testing to confirm genuine silver content.

Step 3: Weighing Your Silver Accurately

Once purity is known, the silver is weighed using calibrated scales. Bullion bars and coins are usually weighed individually, while mixed jewellery or silverware may be grouped by type.

Weight and purity together determine how much fine silver the item contains. Explaining these figures as we go helps you understand how the final value is reached.

Step 4: Applying the Market Price

Silver prices move with international markets. To value silver fairly in Fish Hoek, we reference the current market context and apply it to the purity and weight of your items.

The final offer reflects those market conditions, alongside practical considerations such as handling, refining and resale. This links local valuation to the wider precious-metals market in a way that is easy to follow.

Step 5: Considering Condition and Collectability

Some silver items are valued mainly for their metal content, while others may have additional collector appeal. Proof coins, limited issues or especially well-preserved pieces may need a more nuanced view.
